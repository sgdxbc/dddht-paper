The storage systems do essentially same things to ensure durability.
Store with redundancy.
Detect redundancy loss in time.
Recover expected redundancy level in time.
Durability proved by showing the recovery is always faster than the series of loss arriving at an independently random interval.

The correlated failures affect the part of how redundancy loss.
If it is lost all at once then no solution can recover the data.
So the solution must ensure redundancy does not loss at once no matter what correlated failures happen.

We must consider \emph{all} possible correlated failures in a permissionless system.
Every combination of nodes may fail together, while they may still fail by themselves.
Different combinations may fail with different probabilities.
But generally nothing is impossible, since we can assert nothing about the participant nodes.

The failure generator may assign certain failure domains with particular high failure rates out of two reasons.
The static reason is the inherent failure patterns of the correlated nodes.
The dynamical/adversarial reason is that attackers may exploit system's assumptions on failure domains as vulnerabilities.
The ability of attackers manipulating failure generator varies, the detailed security analysis is out of topic.
The important piece is that the effect of two factors are indistinguishable.
We cannot rely on things like ``it is an honest node but failed out of nature disaster, so it is easier to be tolerated''.

We don't have an explicit model for adversarial/arbitrary faults in this work.
In our proposed protocol there's no coordination, so it (mostly) does not rely on, \eg, whether the interacted nodes behave correctly.
Those faulty behaviors are also covered by correlated failures.

Discussion on authoritative storage systems.
Although we mostly claim that the authoritative storage systems have predetermined failure domains and this work does not contribute to them, failing to identify correlations causes disastrous consequences in those storage systems.
So we suggest modeling faults of authoritative storage systems with our fault model as well, with failure generators adjusted toward expected failure domains, but still leave some uncertainty.

Discussion on correlations.
The standard way to describe correlation is through a \emph{correlation coefficient}, usually denoted with $\rho$, in the range of $[-1, 1]$ where $\rho = 1$ means definite correlated, \ie, the two events always happen/not happen together, and $\rho = -1$ means definite anti-correlated, \ie, there's always exactly one event of the two happens.

The correlation coefficients of node failures in our fault model can be inferred from failure generators.
For each pair of nodes, let $f_{all}$ be the sum of failure rates of the failure domains that contain at least one of the two nodes, $f_{both}$ be the sum of failure rates of the failure domains that contains both of the nodes, then $\rho = f_{both}/f_{all}$.
Notice that the correlation coefficients in our fault mode is always non-negative.
This is expected and matches the observation from previous works \sgd{cite}.

Analysis previous works with proposed fault model.

Design proper failure generators.
A uniformly random generator may be good enough.
The failure rates of failure domains of same sizes are the same.
The failure rates exponentially decrease as the failure domain size grows.
This is not realistic as in the real world a storage node will not possibly fail together with every other node.
But it's hard to make any assumption, and we should design systems that can deal with this.
\lijl{Even for failure domains with the same size, some of them have higher failure rates.
For example, the same number of nodes within the same room vs. nodes across the globe have different correlated failure rate.}

A slightly ``friendly'' generator would be the one above with locality.
Some node pairs are considered to be independent to each other, and every failure domain that contain both of them are removed.
To this end we will eventually end up with the case where all pairs are independent and only failure domains containing single node are retained \ie there are only independent failures left.
This gives us a spectrum of generators to evaluate how systems react to varying correlation degrees.

And there are tailored generators for special cases. \sgd{Not sure whether this is necessary.}

Each previous work is evaluated with redundancy adjusted to different levels.
