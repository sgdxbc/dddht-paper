\begin{abstract}

Distributed hash table (DHT) is commonly used to build decentralized storage networks that do not require bookkeeping metadata and are free from competitive storage nodes.
However, we observe that DHT enforce a uniform distributed workload across the nodes, which may lead to serious node overwhelming in a heterogeneous setup.
This leads to degraded durability in some critical cases, and we show that the classical virtual node approach is not effective.
In this work we propose \dht, a novel DHT that can balance the available capacity of storage nodes and is good at avoid overwhelming storage nodes.
The \dht uses classified \texttt{xor} distance and the power of two choices to achieve differential discoverability, and balances the available capacities of nodes by differentiating their discoverability.
Preliminary results show that \dht can use more than 90\% of storage capacity before overwhelming any storage node and significantly increases durability of the storage system.
\cite{bgp}

\end{abstract}
