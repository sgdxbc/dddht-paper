\begin{abstract}
Building open decentralized storage system across administrative domains benefit end users by decoupling the durability guarantee from the trust to individual operators. However, the permissionless setup also brings its unique challenges of dealing with unpredictable correlated failures. This work focuses on the strong Byzantine fault scenarios where the number of faulty nodes is proportional to the number of entire system (e.g. 1/3). We discuss the durability problem of deploying fixed size placement groups under this system setting, revealing the inability of preventing data loss with previous works. We propose a novel storage solution that tolerates such correlations by disseminating the data blocks to the whole network, and eventually achieves durability under strong Byzantine fault assumptions. And we finally address the challenges of data dissemination in a system-wide scale to make the solution practical in reality. The evaluation shows the performance characteristics of our storage protocol is comparable to existing works while tolerating stronger threat models.
\end{abstract}
