\begin{abstract}

Distributed hash tables (DHTs) are fundamental to decentralized storage networks, handling their data placement strategies.
However, we observe that DHTs typically impose a uniformly distributed workload across all nodes.
Given the heterogeneous storage capacities prevalent in permissionless storage networks, this uniform workload can lead to premature service degradation even when the overall storage utilization is low.
In this paper, we introduce \dht, a new DHT design capable of equalizing the available storage space across nodes, thereby enabling efficient utilization of heterogeneous storage resources.
The design of \dht incorporates a novel classified distance metric and leverages the power of two random choices.
Preliminary results demonstrate that \dht can utilize over 90\% of the total storage capacity before any individual node becomes full, achieving a significant improvement compared to the widely adopted Kademlia DHT.

\end{abstract}
