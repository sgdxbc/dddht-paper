\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Decentralized storage networks (DSNs)~\cite{kubiatowicz2000oceanstore,ratnasamy2001scalable,benet2014ipfs,trautwein2022design,haeberlen2005glacier,filecoin} have garnered considerable interest due to their pivotal role in the ongoing decentralization trend~\cite{bitcoin,ethereum} and substantial advancements.
FileCoin~\cite{filecoin}, a prominent contemporary DSN, involves over 1500 storage nodes that collectively offer more than 22 EB ($\approx 2.2\times10^{10} GB$) of effective storage capacity\footnote{\url{https://filfox.info/en}}.
In 2022, IPFS~\cite{trautwein2022design} documented a daily download volume of 6.57 TB originating from 7.1 million user requests via a single gateway.
Given the substantial user demand and increasing adoption of DSNs as a storage paradigm for diverse applications~\cite{balduf2022dude,vorick2014sia,li2024sok}, guaranteeing high availability and usability constitutes a primary objective for these networks.
% Considering the inherent volatile nature and asynchronous network condition arising from the permissionless characteristic of DSNs, the development of an efficient methodology for organizing storage nodes to \emph{replicate} stored data without centralized orchestration is essential for attaining these attributes.
High availability requires DSNs to \emph{replicate} the data on a group of nodes, \ie, its \emph{placement group}, to mask the data loss due to node failures and churns, while usability requires DSNs to locate placement groups whose nodes are able to handle incoming storing requests.

Distributed hash tables (DHTs)~\cite{maymounkov2002kademlia,chord,rowstron2001pastry}, underpinned by consistent hashing~\cite{chash} as a core principle, are widely adopted in storage architectures for the allocation of data placement groups, designating the storage nodes responsible for data persistence.
% Although centrally managed storage systems~\cite{weil2006ceph,lakshman2010cassandra,decandia2007dynamo} can circumvent the performance degradation and single point of failure associated with a metadata server by directly implementing consistent hashing, decentralized storage networks (DSNs) can only utilize this technique via DHT protocols that encapsulate it.

DHT protocols empower nodes within decentralized networks to identify data placement groups through an iterative online query process.
Consequently, with DHTs, nodes are not required to determine the precise set of currently online peers and their contact information, a challenging endeavor given the permissionless nature and high dynamism of DSN nodes.
DHTs have thus constituted a foundational element of DSNs and are extensively employed for both peer and placement group discovery.

However, \emph{heterogeneity}, an intrinsic characteristic of decentralized systems, introduces a previously overlooked challenge to DSNs.
In contrast to centrally managed storage platforms that typically operate a set of homogeneous storage nodes, DSNs exhibit substantial variation in the capacities of individual storage nodes, spanning from tens of gigabytes to terabyte scales, rendering them heterogeneous.
However, Kademlia~\cite{maymounkov2002kademlia}, the most prevalent DHT protocol employed by Bitcoin~\cite{bitcoin}, Ethereum~\cite{ethereum}, BitTorrent, and IPFS~\cite{benet2014ipfs}, does not account for this heterogeneity.
Within Kademlia, each node receives an equivalent probabilistic storage load, irrespective of its storage capacity and available resources.

As DSNs utilize more and more storage space, the nodes will be exhausted at different rates due to their varying storage capacities.
Concurrently, given the absence of a central controller in DSNs, data cannot be readily migrated away from a full node unless it exits the network; however, the node must remain online to serve its existing data.
Ultimately, DHTs overwhelm smaller nodes even at the low overall utilization and DSNs cannot effectively alleviate the strain on them.

Given that DHTs directly manage massive data placement in most of the DSNs~\cite{kubiatowicz2000oceanstore,ratnasamy2001scalable,haeberlen2005glacier,team2021swarm}, this problem is both real and severe.
% As demonstrated in our simulation in \autoref{sec:bg}, Glacier~\cite{haeberlen2005glacier} rejects new storage requests, leading to the denial of over 90\% of such requests at a mere 10\% overall utilization.
% Conversely, Swarm~\cite{team2021swarm} evicts stored content based on economic valuation, resulting in data loss at only 6\% utilization.

FileCoin~\cite{filecoin} and BitTorrent avoid this issue by using DHTs solely for placing lightweight metadata, consuming very little space on the nodes.
As a result, they can't use DHTs for inherent probabilistic availability guarantees and instead depend on volunteer or economic-driven placement groups.

Base on this observation, we are motivated to optimize the \emph{effective overall utilization} of DSNs.
Specifically, DSNs should maximize the utilization of aggregated storage supply \emph{effectively}: without loading any individual node, thereby preventing diminished usability or availability.
Achieving this objective presents a greater challenge in DSNs compared to centrally managed storage systems, because data placement strategies must avoid overwhelming nodes at the first place for the lack of the capability for real-time reactive adjustments.

While prior research has extensively examined related subjects, none are directly applicable to our problem context: the \emph{heterogeneity} of \emph{storage} within a \emph{permissionless} environment.
The virtual node technique has been explored for rebalancing probabilistic nonuniform workload distribution~\cite{chord} and for allocating weighted workload to heterogeneous nodes.

We contend that virtual nodes break the assumption on per-identity failure domain made by DSNs, causing unexpected correlated failures~\cite{filecoin_loss} and harming availability, thus should be entirely avoided by DSNs.

Furthermore, weighted DHT~\cite{schindelhauer2005weighted} does not respect nodes' available storage space, DHT load balancing schemes~\cite{karger2004simple,misra2004clash} considers nonuniform key access instead of nonuniform storage capacities, and consistent hashing methods for bounded load~\cite{mirrokni2018consistent,chen2021revisiting} are not suitable to apply in permissionless settings.

In this work, we propose \dht\footnote{
    \dht represents Discoverability Differentiable DHT.
    As we retire the concept of differentiating discoverability in a later refinement of the work, a new interpretation of the abbreviation is under investigation.
}, a novel DHT that introduces substantial improvement for utilizing heterogeneous storage resources based on the well-established Kademlia protocol.
To the best of our knowledge, \dht is the first DHT design that can \emph{equalize the available space} across heterogeneous storage nodes, thus maintain storage consumption within capacity limits on \emph{all} storage nodes while maximizing the utilization.
The \dht architecture comprises two elements.
% We provide an intuitive description of the design rationale of \dht and present preliminary findings in \autoref{sec:overview}.
\lijl{We should summarize our main techniques in the intro. The goal should be that anyone can have rough understanding of the system by just reading the intro.} \gd{done.}
\emph{Classified distance} improves the distance metrics of Kademlia to globally direct weighted storage workload to the nodes, proportional to their storage capacities.
We further employ \emph{the power of two random choices} to perform accurate equalization of the available spaces locally between two groups of nodes.
By combining the two elements, \dht accomplishes the goal of equalizing available spaces across the whole network.

Our preliminary evaluation demonstrates the effectiveness of the \dht architecture.
Under the identical workload previously applied to Glacier and Swarm, \dht entirely eliminates overwhelmed nodes and ensures both availability and usability.
% This is attributed to the substantially more balanced distribution of available space across nodes in \dht compared to Kademlia.
In stress simulations, \dht can store data up to nearly 100\% of overall utilization before node overload and data loss occur, representing a 60\% improvement over Kademlia.
In the future we plan to implement \dht in real and perform real world deployment evaluations.

DHTs are effective at balancing request load across nodes in a network.
They, however, make a critical assumption that every node always has sufficient storage capacity to store all mapped objects.
In a permissionless network where participants exhibit significant heterogeneity, such assumption is no longer valid.
Even when there is sufficient aggregated capacity in network, the available capacity (AC) on each node can have large variance due to different initial capacity, request distribution, and lifetime.
We validate this hypothesis using simulations in \autoref{sec:bg:problem}.
Variance in AC directly leads to degraded data availability.
Since DHTs distribute storage requests uniformly across the network, a node with insufficient AC has to either reject a storage request or evict existing objects when other nodes still have spare capacity, resulting in lower redundancy level.
The issue is even more severe when the network scales down (e.g., nodes churning and network partitions).

To address these limitations, we propose Differentiable Discoverability DHT (\dht), a decentralized storage network that augments DHT by \emph{differentiating} workloads on storage nodes based on their available capacities.
Compared to traditional DHTs, \dht ensures balanced available capacities across all storage nodes, regardless of their total capacity, request patterns, or lifetime.
Such property enables strong durability guarantee: the data loss probability can be derived entirely from the redundancy level, the proportion of faulty nodes, and the skewness of storage capacities across the network.
Conversely, given the faulty proportion (similar to existing BFT protocols) and the capacity skewness, we can determine a minimum redundancy level such that the data loss probability is negligible.
\lijl{Is the point that prior DHTs can't derive this redundancy level, or their minimum redundancy level is higher than us?}
This makes \dht the first work that guarantees data availability when operating under permissionless, Byzantine storage nodes with bounded storage size.

Our key insight is to redefine the concept of node \emph{discoverability} in DHT.
While discoverability of a node has been a binary property (whether the node is included in any DHT \ffind), we extend this definition to an ordinal value to represent how \emph{frequent} the node appears in the results of \ffind.
%that the target \vid is uniformly distributed in the \vid's domain space.
In \dht, the discoverability of nodes is proportional to the nodes' AC, \ie, nodes with higher AC are discovered with higher frequency, and thus receive more storage requests.
On the other hand, nodes with low remaining capacity receive few storage requests, reducing the likelihood of forced eviction and data losses.

\dht augments Kademlia~\cite{maymounkov2002kademlia} by introducing two novel designs to differentiate the discoverability of storage nodes with different AC.
The first design applies \emph{macro} differentiation of storage nodes by defining \emph{node classes}: nodes are classified based on their total capacity.
\dht implements this classification by modifying the Kademlia's \texttt{xor} distance definition.
Node classes approximate the nodes' AC with their total capacity.
A weakness of this static approach is that the node class cannot be adjusted based on the current AC.
\lijl{After applying node class, what properties do the system have? What are the remaining ``errors''?}
To compensate such shortcoming, we apply \emph{the power of two choices} (2RC) for \emph{micro} differentiation.
Specifically, each \ffind operation locates two candidate placement groups, and chooses the group with the higher \emph{minimum} AC.
As such, 2RC performs accurate local differentiation but only among a few nodes from the two candidate placement groups.
Combining the two methods results in balanced AC without degrading DHT performance, leading to improved data availability guarantees.
