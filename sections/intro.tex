\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Blockchain scalability issue arises.
For permissionless blockchains, the bottleneck is network.
Result: disseminate smaller data size, propagate to smaller group of nodes.

\emph{Disaggregate} storage: scaling up world state while keep dissemination efficiently.
Leave a stub (\eg content hash) in the state and access content off-chain.
Example: NFT exchange, user-generated content, off-chain AI inference with on-chain verifiable computation proof.

BFT guarantees a consistent and \emph{durable} state.
Durable was implied by full replication in BFT, but now explicitly relies on the disaggregated storage.
The preconditions of BFT security include assumptions made on both the consensus and the storage parts.
Involving centralized storage service would be a major distortion on permissionless blockchains' security model.

Permissionless systems usually consist of abundant devices with moderate hardware.
Restricted network bandwidth/computation power/storage capacity, excluded from BFT protocol, underutilized as \emph{light clients}.
Idea: construct decentralized storage network (DSN) with these devices as \emph{light storage providers}.

Challenges (vs. storage systems with centralized management).
Heterogeneous nodes: various storage capacity, content policy \& lifetime.
Unpredictable node behavior: cannot predict the churning or failure correlation even on the honest ones, not to mention the Byzantine ones.

Two kinds of DSNs.
The ones that storage providers pick what contents to store; either through market and contract (Sia, Filecoin, Züs) or the storage providers can ``mine'' on arbitrary content managed by the DSN (Arweave, EthStorage, Permacoin).
Advantage: storage providers can store as much as they can/want.
Highly competitive storage providers; encouraging large-scale and centralized providers.
Usually have small numbers of storage providers indeed.
Not suitable for \emph{light} storage providers.

The ones that network ``pushes'' to storage providers and storage providers cannot choose what and how much to store (Swarm, Glacier).
The placement is automatically done by DHT primitives.
Friendly for light storage providers.

However, we discover that smaller providers may be saturated long before the whole network and eventually affect durability.
Then those storage providers are forced to evict upon more ingestion or DSN scale down (even just temporarily).
This eventually results in degraded durability.
IPFS only stores metadata in this way, occupy a small portion of disk space and will not run into trouble.
Things are not the same for DSNs that intend to utilize as much disk space as provided.

We argue that the \emph{effective capacity} of such DSNs are much lower than expect \ie the total storage supply divided by the average redundancy.
Because of the limitation of relying on DHT's placement, a significant portion of storage capacity cannot be made use of.
As the result, the \emph{effective storage amplification} is much higher than the \emph{useful} amplification \ie the actual redundancy.

Goals:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Build on light storage providers; algorithmically arrange placement instead of economically.
    Storage providers do not need to compete with each other.
    \item As secure as pure BFT.
    Deliver a probabilistic durability guarantee that only depends on failure/churn rate and the assumptions already made by BFT.
    Most noticeably, keep data alive as long as the overall capacity is considerably higher than demanded (\ie stored data size times redundancy), so matter how the capacity is distributed across the storage providers and even in the presence of DSN scaling down.
    Standard incentive strategies and proof of storage/retrievability can be applied.
    \item Be scalable: make exhaustive use of storage supply.
    It would be trivial to keep a tiny piece of data safe if a huge amount of storage is holding or \emph{ready to be holding} it.
    Maximize storage utilization and keep the storage amplification close to redundancy as long as the contents are still secured.
\end{itemize}

The key idea is to unify \emph{capacity workload frequency} \ie the probability of being allocated for a certain storage capacity.
Currently, DHT protocols guarantee unified \emph{node discoverability}, which can be considered as \emph{node frequency}.
However, on one hand that does not translate to unified capacity frequency as storage providers have different capacities.
On the other hand, the node frequency follows normal distribution.
The normal distribution brings undesirable variance so that in the worst case a node can be \xxx{formula} ``more discoverable'' with high probability.
The placement that relies on the node discoverability is highly affected and far from ideal.
\xxx{State simulation results of Swarm.}

We modified Kademlia DHT into discoverability-differentiable DHT (DDDHT) with the concept of \emph{node class}.
The higher the node class, the more discoverable the node is.
This is implemented by masking the high bits when computing ID distances, where the number of masked bits correspond to the node class.
Effectively, the class 1 nodes have 2 IDs, one is its actual one and the other one with the highest bit flipped, and class 2 nodes have 4 IDs with all combination of the two highest bits, and so on.
As the result, when the query uniformly distributed across the ID space, the probability of a node to be included in the query result doubles as the node class increases by one.
Notice that the high bits of original IDs are preserved even though they may be masked for computing distances.
This ensures ID uniqueness, and it's not possible for query results to include duplicated node contact.

With the DDDHT, storage providers can set their node classes based on their storage capacities.
The most appropriate node class for a storage provider may occasionally change based on the change of the storage capacity of the provider, the number of storage providers, the storage capacity of the whole network and the ingested data size.
Thus, DDDHT further supports changing node class efficiently to prevent wasting storage capacity or overwhelming the storage providers.

DDDHT correctly balance the capacity frequency of storage providers of different capacities.
However, the frequency still follows a wide normal distribution.
We further introduce the power of two choices mechanism to the DHT protocol.
We follow previous works \xxx{cite}, hash contents twice and choose the placement group with the lighter load of the two.
The information about the load of the storage providers is integrated into the DHT protocol.
As the result, the storage providers are assigned workload that is closely matching each other, given that they have the same node class.
