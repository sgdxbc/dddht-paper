\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\begin{outline}
Blockchains are important consensus facilities for decentralized systems.

Challenge: the state size of blockchains overwhelms consensus nodes.

Approach: offload immutable data \ie \emph{contents} to permanent storage and off-chain access.

Notice: even though read-only contents are off consensus path and self-certifiable, they still need to meet the implicit durability requirement expected by decentralized apps.

Where to store then?
Centralized storage is not desirable.
\xxx{Pick a good angle to argue.}

Introduce decentralized storage.
Introduce Filecoin.

Problem with Filecoin: impose high durability requirement on each \emph{individual} participants.
High barriers to entry.
Tricky to design effective incentive when expecting high return to cover the cost.

Observation: much more nodes are available than BFT can scale.
Nowadays (even permissionless) BFTs seek high quality \emph{professional} consensus nodes.
Unreliable devices cannot meet the requirement and only serve as thin clients, and their storage capacity is underutilized.

Goals:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Co-design with BFT.
    Propose \emph{storage client} in addition to (Ethereum's) existing consensus, execution and thin clients.
    Storage clients are like thin clients but hold responsibility of storing contents.
    They don't need to be available 24/7 (unlike consensus clients) but need to be periodically online to produce proofs (unlike thin clients).
    \item The system should be as secure as pure BFT.
    Regarding durability, that means the system can tolerate any up to $f$ portion of storage capacity managed by faulty participants while still ensuring durability of contents referenced by consensus state.
    Durable means retrieving any previously-stored content will eventually success.
    \item Bounded redundancy.
\end{itemize}

Design: efficiently disseminate (encoded fragments of) contents to more than $f+k$ portion of the storage capacity, where the fragments stored by every $k$ portion of the storage capacity can recover the contents.
Periodically attempt to produce \emph{proof of durability} (PoD) for contents: the contents are not only stored with sufficient redundancy, but also the redundancy is properly scattered across more than $f+k$ portion of the storage capacity.
If the attempt fails (either because failures or storage capacity shifts), disseminate the content again.

Notice: not saying it takes $f+k$ portion of the storage capacity to store a content, but every piece \xxx{what is piece?} of that $f+k$ portion contribute a tiny slice of storage to hold one fragment. 
\end{outline}
