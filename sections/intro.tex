\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\begin{outline}
Blockchain scalability issue arises.
For permissionless blockchains, the bottleneck is network.
Result: disseminate smaller data size, propagate to smaller group of nodes.

\emph{Disaggregate} storage: scaling up world state while keep dissemination efficiently.
Leave a stub (\eg content hash) in the state and access content off-chain.
Example: NFT exchange, off-chain AI inference with on-chain verifiable computation proof.

BFT guarantees a consistent and \emph{durable} state.
Durable was implied by full replication in BFT, but now explicitly relies on the disaggregated storage.
So centralized storage service is not a good idea.

Permissionless systems usually consist of abundant devices with moderate hardware.
Restricted network bandwidth/computation power/storage capacity, excluded from BFT protocol, underutilized as \emph{light clients}.
Idea: construct decentralized storage network (DSN) with these devices as storage providers.

Challenges/features (vs. storage systems with centralized management).
Node churns, incur additional repair and more complicated placement management.
Nodes are unreliable in uncertainty: unknown failure rate, unknown correlation.
Byzantine nodes may attempt to cheat the system \eg pretend to store a content or pretend to store more redundancies than what they actually do.

Goals:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Enable devices with moderate hardware to take part in the DSN and contribute to storage capacity.
    Storage providers are not involved in consensus protocol, only infrequently \emph{watch} the updates and \emph{submit} updates to the state.
    \item As secure as pure BFT.
    If under certain situation a pure BFT system is durable, so should be the DSN.
    \item More scalable than BFT.
    Overhead per unit storage remains constant as storing more data, and also remains constant \xxx{or grow in $\log$ speed} as storage supply increases.
    Overhead means both communication overhead for ingesting / retrieval / maintenance and storage overhead \ie redundancy. 
\end{itemize}

Three categories of existing DSNs.
First category (Filecoin, Sia, Züs, etc.) is \emph{static provision}.
Storage providers and clients establish storage contracts through public markets, and the data will always be stored by the certain contracted storage providers.
Push the responsibility of repairing for failures and ensuring durability to \emph{individual} storage nodes.
Consider DSN repairing as last resort, economically discourage DSN-aware failures and repairs to happen.
Not welcome moderate hardware with insufficient durable duration.

Second category (Arweave, EthStorage, Permacoin) approximates \emph{typical blockchains} that a full copy resides on every node.
Storage providers are economically encouraged to keep a copy of as much data as they can.
They usually only reward for storing, not penalize for not storing.
Storage providers must \emph{discover} profitable storing.
Storage providers also usually need to participate consensus.
Not suitable for moderate hardware.

The category we are looking for is \emph{adaptive dynamic sharding} (Swarm).
Each storage provider belongs to a group (usually algorithmically according to its identity).
The storage providers of a group collectively store a shard of data, and repair together for the failures and churning that happen in the shard.
\xxx{Advantage.}
However, the durability is yet to improve.
\xxx{Evidence.}

\xxx{Our design that enhancing adaptive dynamic sharding.}
\end{outline}
